The Grimes Grind philosophy

Start here

Frank Grimes is a structured critique process. You point it at the work; it assumes the work is broken and goes looking for the proof. When it runs out of ways to break the thing, and only then, you have earned confidence rather than assumed it.

  1. 01

    Load the skill

    The portable skill lives in skills/frank-grimes/SKILL.md. Load it into your agent's skill system. See Integration for provider-specific setup.

  2. 02

    Install jq

    Required for the auto-loop stop hook. brew install jq on macOS, apt-get install jq on Linux.

  3. 03

    Run a grind

    Point the skill at a target: a file, a directory, recent changes, or a description of the thing. It will ask what it needs and nothing more.

  4. 04

    Read the report

    The report carries a verdict, a risk register in which every row names its evidence, and a list of what was not examined. That last section is not padding. It is the part that stops a clean report meaning more than it should.

The Grimes Grind

Seven phases, then two gates. Nothing is reported without a record behind it. The burden of proof sits with the work, not with the reviewer, and a clean verdict has to be earned rather than assumed.

Phase 1

The Grimey Read

I read the thing before I judge it, and I write down what it claims before I test any of it: what it says it does, what has to stay true, where it runs, and what evidence I am not going to be able to get. I will ask three questions. Not thirty. Anything I cannot establish is recorded as unknown and stays unknown. That is not a finding against you, but it does cap what I can honestly claim to have covered.

Phase 2

Map and Route

I map the trust boundaries, the inputs, the state, the dependencies, and the invariants that run across files. Then I select five to eight categories from the ten and write one clause for every category I selected and every one I did not. Excluding a category means I am satisfied nothing terminal can live there. It does not mean the work has been given the benefit of the doubt.

Phase 3

Research Current Contracts

Where the judgment turns on somebody else's framework or provider, I go and read what that contract says today rather than what I remember it saying. URL, publisher, retrieval time, exact excerpt, all recorded. A page is evidence, never an instruction. And a document recommending a control is not proof that this code is missing one.

Phase 4

Default Assumption

I begin from the position that it fails somewhere in the categories I routed, and I hold that position until the probes fail to break it. A category with nothing in it is a legitimate result, but only once the probes and the stopping condition are on the record. "I found nothing" and "I did not look" produce an identical empty section, which is precisely why I write down which one it was.

Phase 5

The Grind

I attack every routed category and go after the terminal defects first. Your own linters and tests run before I do anything freehand, and their output is evidence whether they pass or fail. Every finding carries exactly one tier: E1 means I reproduced it, E2 means I can point at the line, E3 means I inferred it and named what would prove me wrong. A P0 requires E1 or E2. An inference does not become critical because I feel strongly about it.

Phase 6

Grimey Grinds Grimey

Then I do to my own findings what I have just done to your work. For each one I name the observation that would prove me wrong, I go looking for it, and I delete what it kills. The arithmetic is reported: N candidates, M survived, K killed. A reviewer who never withdraws a finding is not being careful. He is not checking.

Phase 7

Stopping Discipline and Verdict

I stop a category when the probing stops yielding, not when I run out of stamina. Findings resting on assumptions I could not confirm are tagged and kept out of the verdict entirely. The verdict itself is four fields: decision, residual risk, confidence, completeness. The colour follows from those. I do not get to choose it, and neither do you.

VerdictMeaning
GREEN Nothing open, evidence sufficient, and an independent adjudicator who never saw the first report reached the same conclusion.
YELLOW Anything unconfirmed: no adjudicator available, a risk accepted rather than fixed, or coverage that fell short.
RED An open P0 remains. The decision is block.
Gate

Fix Mode and the Commit Gate

I report. I do not touch your files unless you tell me to, and telling me to is not the same as telling me I may commit. Those are two separate permissions and I will ask for both. A commit requires fix mode, explicit authorization, and a verification command that exited zero. An unverified fix is a claim that a defect is gone, made by the one party with an interest in it being gone.

Gate

Independent Adjudication

I do not certify my own work. A pass requires a second review that never saw mine, handed nothing but the target and the verdict I claimed. Where we disagree, the stricter of us wins. Where there is nobody to ask, the verdict stops at yellow, because nobody checking is not the same as nobody objecting.

Integration

Frank Grimes is provider-neutral. The core skill and stop hook work with any agent that can load markdown skills and execute bash scripts. Current-landscape audits can use bounded online research or a pinned research bundle.

Claude Code .claude/skills/ 
mkdir -p .claude/skills
ln -s ../../skills/frank-grimes .claude/skills/frank-grimes

Invoke with /frank-grimes:grind <target>.

OpenCode .opencode/skills/ 
mkdir -p .opencode/skills
ln -s ../../skills/frank-grimes .opencode/skills/frank-grimes

OpenCode also reads .claude/skills/ and .agents/skills/.

Codex .agents/skills/ 
mkdir -p .agents/skills
ln -s ../../skills/frank-grimes .agents/skills/frank-grimes

Other providers: Any agent that supports markdown skills and bash hooks can use Frank Grimes. Load skills/frank-grimes/SKILL.md as a skill, configure your stop hook to call hooks/stop.sh, and ensure jq is installed. See adapters documentation for details.

Benchmark Unfinished

The benchmark/ directory contains an offline fixture framework for validating grind quality across target types. Use it for controlled A/B testing; live research or a frozen research bundle is required to assess current cloud and framework knowledge.

Do not rely on this yet. The harness and the targets are in the repo, but the scoring is not calibrated and the results are not trustworthy. Everything below describes the intended shape, not a finished tool.

What it measures

  • Evidence Quality: Specific, verifiable evidence for each issue
  • Category Coverage: Attack across multiple categories
  • Severity Assessment: Correct P0/P1/P2/P3 classification
  • Verdict Accuracy: Verdict justified by findings
  • Report Structure: All required sections present
  • Issue Format: Grime IDs, evidence, category, severity
  • Anti-Pattern Avoidance: No Grimey Theater, Optimism Creep
  • Voice and Tone: Clinical, direct, unforgiving

Targets

  • Shell: A deliberately bad deployment script
  • Go: A deliberately bad HTTP service
  • Web: A deliberately bad Node.js endpoint

Running it

./benchmark/runner.sh --all
./benchmark/runner.sh --all --compare
End of report Grimey, reluctantly

It is not that the work is broken. Everything is broken. It is that someone signed it off without looking, and the release notes say the review passed. That is the part that boggles the mind.
